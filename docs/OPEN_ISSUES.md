## The six conventions

**1. Deepening ≠ entry.** Your earlier ruling (CP §6.2) was "code ALL EU
accessions meeting R1–R3." When I applied that literally, Spain 1986 came
up: Spain was already coded *inside* the US sphere via E002 (1953). R1 for
an entry requires entering a *new* sphere — and joining the EEC doesn't
move Spain anywhere; it deepens a membership that exists. So I ruled that
accessions by insiders fail R1 and coded no episode for ESP 1986, GRC 1981,
PRT 1986, DNK/IRL/GBR 1973, AUT/SWE 1995, CYP 2004, NOR-EEA 1994. The
conflict: this looks like it narrows your "code all EU accessions" ruling —
I argue it merely applies R1 as written, but you should confirm that's what
you meant. There's also a real asymmetry I want you to see: I claimed
AUT/SWE/IRL were "inside the Western economic order since OEEC 1948" to
reject their accessions — yet I did **not** give them formation-block rows
(E022–E033 covers only NATO founders + GRC + DEU). Strict consistency says
they should get economic-order formation entries [1947,1949] (F2+F3, no
F1), the same subtype as FIN's E058. I'd recommend adding those 3–4 rows;
the alternative is declaring OEEC too thin for membership, which would
re-open AUT/SWE/IRL 1973–95 as entries.

**2. Succession ≠ severance.** Slovenia and Croatia exited *Yugoslavia*,
not a sphere — Yugoslavia had been non-aligned since 1948 (E001). The
tempting alternative is to code a 1991 "severance from the federation," but
that misclassifies on the theory's own terms: intra-federal transfers ran
*from* Slovenia and Croatia *to* the poorer republics, so a severance
coding would attach H2's sign prediction to flows going the wrong
direction. Their collapses (HRV −33.5%, war) are recorded as context. The
conflict to rule on: are you comfortable that the 1991 breakups stay out of
the Severance regressor even though they're the largest political ruptures
in those countries' histories?

**3. Window-edge formation blocks.** The 1945–49 episodes can never be
tested the way later ones can: there is no pre-1945 growth regime in the
panel, so the acceleration test (A3 compares against a prior regime) is
undefined for them. Options were (a) don't code them, treat 1945–49 as
initial conditions; or (b) code them for a complete sphere record but
pre-register their exclusion from episode-level outcome counts. I chose
(b) — and registered it *now*, before Step 6, precisely so it can't later
look like selection after seeing results. Your Phase A answer leaned this
way but this is the concrete decision point; if you prefer (a), I delete
19 rows.

**4. R4 extended to imposed entries.** The protocol's R4 (voluntariness)
was written for severances: imposed severances carry causal weight because
chosen exits may be endogenous. The Soviet-bloc 1945–49 cases forced the
issue — those entries happened under military occupation and rigged
elections, i.e., they are exogenous to the country's economic conditions,
which is exactly the property R4 exists to capture. So I extended the class
symmetrically. The conflict: this is a protocol amendment, and it has
teeth — it means ROU's post-entry collapse (D070, −36.7%) could be used as
causal evidence later rather than dismissed as endogenous. ALB is the
in-block counterexample (chosen entry, no occupation), so the boundary is
visible.

**5. R5=0 entries outside the H2 sign test.** H2 as written predicts the
sign for entry into a *richer* sphere. The Soviet-bloc entries were entries
into a *poorer* sphere (USSR 4,181 vs Europe median 5,426 in 1949). Two
readings: (a) they're outside H2's domain — my choice, so they're coded but
excluded from the sign test; (b) H2 generalizes symmetrically,
poorer-sphere entry predicts deceleration — and ROU D070 would then
*confirm* it. I recorded D070 "neutrally, no conclusion drawn" because (b)
expands the hypothesis post hoc. But this is a theory question, not a
coding question — it's genuinely yours to decide, and it should be decided
before Step 6, not after seeing the counts.

**6. Entry consummation rule.** When is an entry done? I ruled: association
(F2/F3) + security guarantee (F1) = consummated; association alone =
unconsummated. Consequences: ALB and HRV consummate at NATO accession (Apr
2009) even though ALB's EU track is still unfinished, while UKR/GEO/MDA —
association agreements but no NATO — stay unconsummated. The alternative
(consummation at EU accession) would make ALB's entry incomplete to this
day and move HRV's endpoint to 2013. Note there's a second track embedded
here: economic-order entries with no F1 ever (FIN, MLT, the E009 Vietnam
subtype) consummate at the economic act itself. That two-track structure
predates this batch (E009) but I'm now making it explicit — confirm you
want NATO, not Brussels, to be the point of no return for the
security-track cases, which is what the theory's emphasis on security
guarantees implies.

## The case-level pending items

**E059 MLT (medium confidence).** The conflict in one line: Malta had an
EEC Association Agreement from 1970 — and by the CYP logic (association =
already inside → deepening), Malta 2004 should be deepening too. I coded it
an entry because Malta made a discrete *exit move* in 1971–87 (base closure
1979, Libyan line, constitutional neutrality) and a discrete *return* in
1987–90, whereas Cyprus never left. If you think the association agreement
means Malta never actually exited, E059 should be deleted and Malta joins
the deepening list; if you think Cyprus's non-alignment was also a real
distance, CYP should get an entry episode instead. Three coherent
positions, I picked the middle one.

**E020 AZE (medium, carried over).** The security record says severed: no
Russian bases from 1991, CST joined 1993 then non-renewed 1999, GUAM, oil
reoriented West. But it never left the CIS (F3 intact), and the UN
validation *fails* — its voting distance to Russia *fell* 1992→2000 (Aliyev
balancing). Per the E009 convention, validation failures are published, not
fatal, and the material record dominates — hence my episode coding. The
equally defensible alternative: sub-threshold gradual exit, no episode (the
UZB treatment). This is a judgment about which evidence class wins when
they conflict.

**MDA and UZB (no-episode determinations).** These are the same boundary
problem as AZE, decided the other way, and they should be reviewed together
because consistency across AZE/GEO/UZB/MDA is exactly what's at stake. MDA:
the government declared independence and never joined the CST, but Russian
troops never left Transnistria — I ruled the severance unconsummated
(patron vetoed it), so no episode. UZB: oscillation — left the CST 1999,
joined GUAM, then re-converged (rejoined CSTO 2006) without any economic
reorientation — single-family moves only, below the 2-family threshold. The
live question: if you keep E020 AZE on the material record, UZB's 1999–2006
window differs only in degree (it reversed), not in kind — are you
comfortable that "reversal" is the discriminating criterion?

## Batch 3 additions (Latin America, logged 2026-11-03)

**7. Pre-existing informal sphere membership at the window edge = roster
initial condition, not an episode.** The 1947–48 Rio/OAS acts
institutionalized a US sphere that had existed informally since the early
20th century (F1 continuity documented: Act of Chapultepec 1945 → Rio
1947 → OAS Charter 1948; trade dominance already in place). Twenty screen
candidates resolved as initial condition "USA sphere from 1945", zero
episodes — the mirror of convention 3. The conflict: in Europe the
formation acts constituted *new* memberships and WERE coded as episodes;
here the membership pre-existed. If you rule that formalization itself is
the codable entry act, 20 rows must be added — but then CUB's E003
severance (1960) would sever from a 1947 entry rather than from the
long-standing membership it presupposes, and consistency with the
deepening convention (ESP 1986) breaks.

**8. Threat-episode threshold.** severance_threat rows (E017 class) are
coded only where a documented, government-level move toward exit is
corroborated by independent evidence (CHL 1970–73 coded E068: UN
divergence + GSDB destabilization record). Weaker drifts — PER 1968–75
(Soviet arms + NAM), GUY 1966–85, SUR 1982–83, GTM 1951–54 — are
documented in the batch note without threat rows. The conflict: a looser
threshold adds negative controls (more power for robustness) but dilutes
the class; a stricter one keeps it clean. Only CHL 1970–73 met the bar in
this batch.

**9. F4 (patron coercion) is not itself an R2 family.** US embargoes,
destabilization and coup support against its own clients are hierarchy
enforcement (Lake), not severance, unless F1/F2/F3 ties actually
terminate. Applied to reject DOM 1960–64, HTI 1986–87/2000–02, CRI
1999–2003, PAN 1987–89, ARG 1982. Was implicit in earlier coding; made
explicit because Latin America is dense with US coercion and the rule
does real work here.

**E067 GUY [1985,1991] — medium confidence.** Entry coded on Hoyte's
reorientation + OAS accession Jan 1991, UN-validated (GUY–USSR
0.01→1.32). The conflict: Guyana's 1966–85 non-alignment is
non-membership, but Western economic ties persisted throughout (IMF
program 1978; bauxite to Western markets) — so the case sits on the
borderline with the deepening convention. If you rule the economic-order
membership was never interrupted, E067 should be downgraded to deepening
(no episode) and Guyana's whole 1945–2019 record becomes initial
condition + context.
