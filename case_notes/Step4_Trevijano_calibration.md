# Step 4 calibration — Spain, United Kingdom, United States, France

**Status:** calibration determinations, user review requested; not merged into the panel.
PS-FRA-02 (France 1986–88) is conditional pending consistent organizational-integration
evidence for its party-financing component.
**Window:** 1945–2019. **Coder:** assistant. **Sequence:** CCP and documentary/legal
records were evaluated before the García-Trevijano comparison. No economic outcome,
V-Dem, or Polity evidence was used.

**Scope guard.** This note resolves the formal anchors and the effective-power and
apparatus clusters capable of meeting the Step 4 thresholds. It is not the final
country-year census of every routine statute or CCP amendment. Positive codes below
are calibration episodes; rejected candidates and their reasons remain explicit.

## Rules applied

- `RegimeChange`: an authoritative change in the rules governing access to or
  organization of power. A codified constitution is not necessary.
- `PowerStructure`: effective change in >=2 distinct dimensions within three calendar
  years: (a) separation/concentration; (b) independence of representation;
  (c) party–state fusion; (d) control of rulers. Persistence and routine operation of
  an existing rule are not new transformations. A positive code does not imply
  democratic completion or arrival at Trevijano's republican ideal.
- Franchise expansion is not automatically independence of representation; vertical
  devolution is not automatically a change in existing national representation.
- Public financing supports party–state fusion only when the record demonstrates
  durable integration of permanent party organizations into the state. Candidate
  subsidies, election-cost reimbursement, and campaign regulation do not qualify
  automatically, and the same test applies across countries.
- Apparatus restructuring: effective change in >=2 distinct components among
  coercive, administrative, judicial, and fiscal machinery within three calendar years.
  The record must show transfer, consolidation, abolition, or politicization of durable
  functions and authority, not merely renaming or routine agency creation.
- The principal effective date is the year the second qualifying dimension/component
  becomes effective; the full interval remains recorded. One act cannot satisfy two
  dimensions/components merely by relabelling.

## 1. Spain

### Baseline and formal record

The Franco order was a documented constitutional order composed of the Fundamental
Laws, not a constitutional vacuum. The 1945 Fuero/Cortes arrangements, 1947 Law of
Succession, 1958 Principles of the National Movement, and 1967 Organic Law of the
State changed formal organization without introducing competitive control of rulers.
CCP records these as events in 1945, 1946, 1958, and 1967; the precise content of each
must remain in the formal-event ledger.

The transition contains two distinct formal anchors: Law 1/1977 for Political Reform
and the 1978 Constitution. The former replaced the existing Cortes with an elected
bicameral legislature and prescribed universal, direct, and secret election; the latter
replaced the Fundamental Laws with a new formal order. The 1992 amendment concerns
European electoral rights; the 2011 balanced-budget amendment does not change access
to or organization of political power and is rejected as `RegimeChange` under the
protocol, notwithstanding its fiscal-policy importance.

Primary anchors:
- [Law 1/1977 for Political Reform](https://www.boe.es/buscar/doc.php?id=BOE-A-1977-165),
  especially arts. 1–2 and transitory provision 1.
- [Royal Decree-Law 12/1977 on political association](https://www.boe.es/buscar/doc.php?id=BOE-A-1977-3663).
- [Electoral Rules, Royal Decree-Law 20/1977](https://www.boe.es/buscar/doc.php?id=BOE-A-1977-7445).
- [1978 Constitution](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1978-31229), including
  the repeal provision and arts. 1, 66–68, 97–108, and 117–127.

### Independent effective-power determination

**Accepted: PS-ESP-01, interval 1976–1978, threshold year 1977. High confidence.**

- **Dimension (c), party–state relation:** the state-integrated National Movement and
  single-party order was replaced by legally competing, state-integrated parties.
  This is a change from a one-party state to a party-state oligarchy, not a claim that
  party and state became separate. Law 21/1976 and Royal Decree-Law 12/1977 legalized
  political association; the June 1977 election made the change operative.
- **Dimension (d), control of rulers:** the pre-existing order did not permit voters or
  rival elites to replace the government through competitive national elections. The
  1977 rules created that capacity. The subsequent UCD-to-PSOE alternation in 1982
  confirms that it was operative, but is corroboration, not the event date.
- **Dimension (b), representation:** removal of opposition exclusion and the creation
  of elected territorial Cortes are also documented. The provincial party-list system
  did not create Trevijano-style uninominal representation; dimension (b) is therefore
  recorded as a change toward electoral representation, not as full independence of
  representatives. It is not needed to defend the threshold because (c)+(d) suffice.
- **Dimension (a):** not used as a qualifying dimension. The 1978 text formally
  separates institutions, but formal enactment alone cannot establish that executive,
  legislative, and judicial power became effectively separated.

The code therefore means **effective transformation, incomplete and directionally
mixed**, not achievement of a democratic republic or complete separation of powers.

### Apparatus determination

**Accepted: AR-ESP-01, 1977. High confidence.**

Royal Decree 1558/1977 reorganized central administration, abolished the separate
Army, Navy, and Air ministries, and created a unified Ministry of Defence. The same
decree also reorganized central administrative departments. Royal Decree 1973/1977
subsequently reorganized the Ministry of Finance, including budget, treasury, tax
administration, customs, inspection, and fiscal-information machinery.

- Coercive: unified Ministry of Defence.
- Administrative: central-government reorganization.
- Fiscal: durable Finance Ministry and tax/budget administration restructuring.

Sources:
- [Royal Decree 1558/1977](https://www.boe.es/buscar/doc.php?id=BOE-A-1977-15200).
- [Royal Decree 1973/1977](https://www.boe.es/buscar/doc.php?id=BOE-A-1977-18220).
- [Law 50/1977 on urgent fiscal reform](https://www.boe.es/buscar/doc.php?id=BOE-A-1977-27150)
  is corroborating context; tax-policy content alone is not the apparatus evidence.

### Contrast with García-Trevijano

Trevijano describes the transition as movement “from the State of one party to the
State of several,” from Franco's dictatorship to an oligarchy of state parties. He
argues that the essential absence of representation, separation, and popular control
continued; he distinguishes alternation among parties from democratic control. His
assessment is explicit in the
[2005 interview](https://www.foroporlamemoria.info/documentos/2005/trevi_15112005.htm).

**Agreement:** the formal order changed; party-state fusion was not abolished; the
result was not Trevijano's democratic republic.

**Contrast:** his conclusion that “the essential” did not change is a judgment about
the resulting level of political freedom. The protocol asks whether the effective
organization changed, without requiring democratic completion. Legalized competition,
party pluralization, and the new capacity for electoral alternation meet (c)+(d) even
though representation remained party-mediated and effective separation remained
defective. Thus PS-ESP-01 is compatible with his structural criticism but is more
sensitive to partial change. It must never be rewritten as “Spain became a democracy
in Trevijano's sense.”

## 2. United Kingdom

### Baseline and formal record

The baseline is the Westminster order: statutes, constitutional conventions,
parliamentary sovereignty, territorial election of MPs, executive dependence on the
Commons, and the judicial functions of the Lords. The absence of a single codified
constitution is not missing evidence and does not make CCP `non-event` dispositive.

Formal qualifying anchors include the Representation of the People Act 1948,
Parliament Act 1949, Life Peerages Act 1958, Peerage Act 1963, Representation of the
People Act 1969, European Communities Act 1972, devolution and Human Rights Acts of
1998, House of Lords Act 1999, Constitutional Reform Act 2005, and Fixed-term
Parliaments Act 2011. These are not automatically power-structure transformations.
Consolidation, technical electoral administration, and changes unrelated to access or
organization remain excluded.

### Independent effective-power determination

**Accepted: PS-GBR-01, interval 1948–1950, threshold year 1950. Medium-high confidence.**

- **Dimension (b):** the 1948 Act abolished university constituencies, remaining plural
  voting, and the remaining double-member seats. This strengthened equal territorial
  representation and eliminated non-territorial university representation. The change
  was operative in the 1950 general election, which supplies the effective threshold
  date rather than merely the 1948 promulgation date.
- **Dimension (a):** the Parliament Act 1949 reduced the Lords' delaying power from two
  years to one. This changed effective inter-cameral checking and further concentrated
  legislative authority in the Commons. It is a transformation in the concentration
  direction, not an improvement in separation.

Sources:
- UK Parliament, [constitutional-change timeline](https://researchbriefings.files.parliament.uk/documents/SN06256/SN06256.pdf).
- Hansard, [1948 Representation of the People Bill](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1948/jun/23/representation-of-the-people-bill).
- UK Parliament, [Parliament Acts and Lords reform](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/evolutionofparliament/houseoflords/parliamentacts/keydates/).

**Rejected: 1998–2000 as a separate power-structure episode. Medium confidence.**

- The Human Rights Act 1998, fully operative in 2000, created a new judicial mechanism
  but preserved the validity and operation of primary legislation after a declaration
  of incompatibility. It is recorded under (a), without inferring a separate (d)
  transformation by relabelling the same mechanism.
- Devolution created new territorial institutions, but did not alter the independence
  of Westminster representation. Vertical reallocation is not automatically dimension
  (b). The Scotland Act is therefore formal and apparatus-relevant evidence, not used
  here as a second power dimension.
- The House of Lords Act 1999 changed membership, but did not establish direct or
  independent electoral representation. It is not counted as a second (b) change.

Primary anchors:
- [Human Rights Act 1998, s.4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1998/42/section/4):
  a declaration does not invalidate the legislation.
- [Scotland Act 1998](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1998/46).
- [House of Lords Act 1999](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/evolutionofparliament/houseoflords/house-of-lords-reform/overview/hereditarypeersremoved/).

**Rejected as power-structure transformations:** the 2005–09 judicial separation is
one dimension (a); the 2011 fixed-term rules are one dimension (d). No second distinct
dimension in the corresponding window was established.

### Apparatus determination

**Accepted: AR-GBR-01, interval 2005–2009, threshold year 2007. High confidence.**

- Judicial: the Constitutional Reform Act 2005 modified the Lord Chancellor's role,
  created the Judicial Appointments Commission, and provided for a separate Supreme
  Court. Implementation was staged: the Commission began operating in 2006 and the
  Supreme Court opened in October 2009.
- Administrative: the Secretary of State for Justice Order 2007 established the
  Ministry of Justice and transferred departmental functions, property, prisons, and
  probation responsibilities.

The second component became effective in 2007; the 2009 Supreme Court date is retained
as the consummation of the judicial reorganization, not allowed to move the panel date.

Sources:
- [Constitutional Reform Act 2005 explanatory notes](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/4/notes/division/2).
- [Secretary of State for Justice Order 2007](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2007/2128/made).
- UK Parliament, [key dates](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/evolutionofparliament/houseoflords/parliamentacts/keydates/).

### Contrast with García-Trevijano

In his [30 August 2013 discussion of UK representation](https://www.antoniogarciatrevijano.info/audios/2320074/),
Trevijano argues that the UK has genuine district representation and effective
political control, but not separation of powers: the executive emerges from and
depends on Parliament. He explicitly contrasts this with the US and treats a Commons
vote against Cameron as evidence of representative control, not separation.

**Agreement:** Westminster representation is real; the system is not presidentially
separated; effective control can operate without Trevijano-style separation.

**Contrast:** PS-GBR-01 records the 1948–50 changes in representation and inter-cameral
power without claiming that the UK became democratic in his sense. His analysis is a
structural assessment of the continuing order, not a denial of those reforms. The 1949
Parliament Act actually illustrates movement toward Commons concentration, which is
consistent with his diagnosis of absent executive–legislative separation.

No direct Trevijano assessment of the 1948–50 or 2005–07 episodes was located; the
contrast is therefore between his explicit structural assessment and my event coding,
not an invented event-specific quotation.

## 3. United States

### Baseline and formal record

The baseline is the written Constitution: separately elected executive and Congress,
territorial election of representatives, federalism, and judicial review. CCP records
six amendments in the window. The 22nd (1951), 23rd (1961), 24th (1964), 25th (1967),
and 26th (1971) qualify as formal changes in control/access/succession. The 27th (1992)
concerns the timing of congressional compensation and is rejected as not changing
access to or organization of authority.

Primary text: US National Archives,
[amendments 11–27](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/amendments-11-27).

Statutory candidates separately include the Voting Rights Act 1965, War Powers
Resolution 1973, and Congressional Budget and Impoundment Control Act 1974. They may
be formal changes without producing a two-dimension transformation.

### Independent effective-power determination

**No qualifying power-structure episode accepted in the calibration record.
Medium-high confidence.**

- 1951 presidential term limits: dimension (d) only.
- 1961 DC electors, 1964 poll-tax prohibition, 1965 voting-rights enforcement, and
  1971 voting age: changes in access/representation, not separate transformations of
  separation, party–state relations, and control.
- 1967 presidential inability and succession: a formal succession rule; not relabelled
  as a new capacity to control rulers under (d).
- 1973 War Powers and 1974 budget legislation: both concern legislative–executive
  balance, dimension (a), and cannot supply two dimensions.
- Watergate and Nixon's resignation demonstrate operation of existing checks; no new
  rule or capacity is inferred from the crisis itself.
- Public campaign financing (1971/1974) was reviewed as a possible party–state change.
  It is rejected here because candidate subsidies and spending rules alone do not
  establish a ruling-party capture of the state or a new party–state organizational
  fusion. This rejection must be revisited if the full sweep adopts a broader rule;
  it cannot be applied inconsistently to France.

Sources for the principal negative distinctions:
- National Archives amendments, above.
- Congress, [history of the 1974 budget process](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R46240).
- FEC, [public-funding history](https://www.fec.gov/about/fec-historical-timeline/).

### Apparatus determinations

**Accepted: AR-USA-01, interval 1947–1949, threshold year 1949. High confidence.**

- Coercive: the National Security Act 1947 created the National Military Establishment,
  National Security Council, and CIA, restructuring defense and intelligence.
- Administrative: the Federal Property and Administrative Services Act 1949 created
  GSA and consolidated administrative support functions.

Sources:
- National Archives, [National Security Act record](https://www.archives.gov/research/foreign-policy/cold-war/ref-info-paper-107).
- GSA, [1949 establishment](https://www.gsa.gov/about-us/organization/office-of-the-administrator/past-and-present-administrators).

**Accepted: AR-USA-02, interval 1977–1978, threshold year 1978. High confidence.**

- Administrative: the Department of Energy Organization Act 1977 consolidated energy
  agencies and created a cabinet department, effective 1 October 1977.
- Judicial: the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act 1978 created a durable court for
  reviewing executive surveillance applications.

Sources:
- Department of Energy, [1977 reorganization](https://www.energy.gov/management/august-4-1977-president-carter-signs-department-energy-organization-act).
- Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Court, [court history](https://www.fisc.uscourts.gov/about-foreign-intelligence-surveillance-court).

These apparatus findings do not imply power-structure transformation; the machinery
changed while the effective constitutional structure continued.

### Contrast with García-Trevijano

Trevijano treats the US as the clear case combining territorial representation with
separation in the origin of executive and legislative power. In the
[2013 UK/US comparison](https://www.antoniogarciatrevijano.info/audios/2320074/), he
contrasts Obama's direct popular election with Cameron's parliamentary selection. In
the [2005 interview](https://www.foroporlamemoria.info/documentos/2005/trevi_15112005.htm),
he rejects the claim that a US president is merely an elected dictator.

**Agreement:** the core power structure was already present; no democratic founding or
transition should be inferred from postwar amendments. Formal and apparatus changes
are compatible with structural continuity.

**Contrast:** none at the structural level. Trevijano's assessment validates the
negative power-structure result, not the specific apparatus episodes, which he does
not directly evaluate in the sources reviewed.

## 4. France

### Baseline and formal record

The Fourth Republic's 1946 Constitution is the baseline. Its party-list parliamentary
order and governmental instability were replaced formally by the 1958 Constitution.
The 1962 amendment introduced direct universal election of the president. The 1974
revision expanded access to constitutional review; the 2000 revision shortened the
presidential term; and the 2008 revision changed executive, parliamentary, judicial,
and referendum rules. EU-related, territorial, and technical amendments require
content review and cannot be assumed to be effective transformations.

Primary anchors:
- Élysée, [1958 Constitution and institutional history](https://www.elysee.fr/en/french-presidency/constitution-of-4-october-1958).
- Conseil constitutionnel, [1962 revision](https://www.conseil-constitutionnel.fr/les-revisions-constitutionnelles/loi-n-62-1292-du-6-novembre-1962).
- Conseil constitutionnel, [constitutional revision chronology](https://www.conseil-constitutionnel.fr/la-constitution/quand-la-constitution-a-t-elle-ete-modifiee).
- Conseil constitutionnel, [2008 constitutional law](https://www.conseil-constitutionnel.fr/les-revisions-constitutionnelles/loi-constitutionnelle-n-2008-724-du-23-juillet-2008).

### Independent effective-power determinations

**Accepted: PS-FRA-01, 1958. High confidence.**

- **Dimension (a):** the 1958 settlement strengthened the executive and constrained
  parliament's ability to overthrow the government, changing effective executive–
  legislative relations.
- **Dimension (b):** the 1958 electoral settlement restored direct, uninominal,
  two-round election of deputies in territorial districts, replacing the Fourth
  Republic's party-list representation.

The 1962 direct presidential election is recorded as a later change in control/access,
not needed to establish the 1958 threshold and not inflated into another two-dimension
episode. The Élysée history and the Assembly's electoral record independently support
the distinction.

**Rejected as a separate threshold episode: 1971–1974.** The 1971 Liberté d'association
decision expanded effective constitutional review; the 1974 revision allowed 60
 deputies or senators to refer legislation. Both strengthen checking under (a), but
remain one dimension. The voting-age change is not relabelled as independence of
representation. Source: Conseil constitutionnel,
[Decision 71-44 DC](https://www.conseil-constitutionnel.fr/en/decision/1971/7144DC.htm)
and [parliamentary referral under Article 61](https://www.conseil-constitutionnel.fr/nouveaux-cahiers-du-conseil-constitutionnel/la-saisine-parlementaire-au-titre-de-l-article-61-de-la-constitution).

**Conditional/provisional: PS-FRA-02, interval 1986–1988, threshold year 1988.**

- **Dimension (b):** proportional party-list election was used in 1986, displacing the
  existing uninominal system; the uninominal two-round system was restored for 1988.
  The effective change and its reversal are both recorded rather than treated as
  persistence.
- **Dimension (c), unresolved:** Law 88-227 of 11 March 1988 introduced systematic public
  financing of political parties, linked to election results and parliamentary
  representation. Under the clarified protocol, this supports fusion only if the
  evidence demonstrates durable integration of permanent party organizations into the
  state. The financing record alone is insufficient. The episode therefore does not
  enter the accepted calibration set unless that organizational-integration evidence
  is established. The identical test applies to US public campaign financing.

Sources:
- National Assembly, [Fifth Republic legislatures and electoral systems](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/histoire/leg5repnew.asp).
- Légifrance, [Law 88-227](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000000321646).
- Vie publique, [history of party financing](https://www.vie-publique.fr/fiches/24004-comment-fonctionne-le-financement-des-partis-politiques).

**Accepted: PS-FRA-03, interval 2008–2009, threshold year 2009. High confidence.**

- **Dimension (d):** Article 3 of the 2008 law inserted a two-consecutive-term limit
  into Article 6, changing control over presidential continuity.
- **Dimension (a):** the reform strengthened parliamentary agenda-setting, scrutiny,
  opposition/minority rights, military-intervention authorization, and review of
  exceptional powers. These are substantively different from the presidential term
  limit, not a relabelling of one consequence.
- Article 46 makes the key parliamentary provisions effective on 1 March 2009. Thus
  the formal regime event is 2008, but the second effective dimension and principal
  power-structure date are 2009. QPC implementation in 2010 is additional context,
  not needed for the threshold.

### Apparatus determination

**Accepted: AR-FRA-01, interval 1958–1959, threshold year 1959. Medium-high confidence.**

- Judicial: Ordinance 58-1270 of 22 December 1958 established the organic statute of
  the judiciary, including the organization and status of judges and prosecutors.
- Fiscal: Ordinance 59-2 of 2 January 1959 established the organic budget-law framework,
  restructuring durable fiscal procedures and institutions.

Primary identifiers are recorded in Légifrance's continuing legal record:
[Ordinance 58-1270](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/ceta/id/CETATEXT000007656988) and
[Ordinance 59-2](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/ceta/id/CETATEXT000007435570).
The code concerns machinery, not the substantive policy of any budget.

No apparatus episode is inferred merely from the 2008 constitutional amendments;
changes in rules and institutional machinery remain separate.

### Contrast with García-Trevijano

Trevijano calls France a quasi- or semi-democracy. In his
[direct assessment](https://www.antoniogarciatrevijano.info/videos/JDuXGSBOxsE/), he
credits uninominal district representation but denies complete separation because the
president's government and program remain dependent on the Assembly. In his
[longer discussion](https://www.antoniogarciatrevijano.info/videos/UuZkRaq7z1k/), he
attributes the unfinished character of the reform to de Gaulle's institutional design.

**Agreement:** 1958 corrected representation and restructured executive–legislative
relations; 1962 did not complete separation; the resulting system is not a full
Trevijano-style democracy. My positive transformations are partial changes, not a
claim of democratic completion.

**Party–state comparison:** the 1988 public-finance law is relevant to the party–state
mechanism emphasized in Trevijano-inspired analysis, but does not by itself resolve
organizational integration under the clarified coding rule. The separate 2020 MCRC series,
[part I](https://www.mcrc.es/post/por-que-en-francia-tampoco-hay-democracia-i-de-iii),
[part II](https://www.mcrc.es/post/por-que-en-francia-tampoco-hay-democracia-ii), and
[part III](https://www.mcrc.es/post/por-que-en-francia-tampoco-hay-democracia-iii),
applies his criteria to contemporary France. Those articles are by Eñaut Uruburu
Martínez, not Trevijano himself, and are therefore treated as secondary comparison,
not as direct Trevijano quotations.

**2008–09:** no direct Trevijano evaluation was located. My positive code does not
contradict his structural assessment because the reform did not create separation in
origin between president and Assembly; it strengthened checks and control within the
existing order.

## Operational clarifications registered after calibration

1. **Partial versus complete transformation.** The binary rule detects effective change,
   not arrival at Trevijano's democratic ideal. Case notes must state this explicitly.
2. **Party financing.** Systematic state support can contribute to dimension (c) only
   when durable integration of permanent party organizations into the state is
   demonstrated. France 1986–88 remains conditional because that evidence is unresolved;
   US candidate subsidies face the identical test and were not accepted as fusion.
3. **Vertical devolution.** UK devolution was not counted as independence of existing
   Westminster representation. Vertical reallocation alone is not dimension (b).
4. **Formal versus effective dates.** UK 1948/1950 and France 2008/2009 demonstrate why
   promulgation and implementation must remain separate.
5. **Normal checks versus new control.** US Watergate and UK parliamentary resistance
   are evidence of existing control, not new transformation events.
6. **Apparatus breadth.** Ministry creation counts only with an actual transfer,
   consolidation, abolition, or politicization of durable functions and authority;
   it cannot be used to infer power transformation.
