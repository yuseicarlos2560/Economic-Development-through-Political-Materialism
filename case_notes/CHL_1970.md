## Why a threat episode, not a severance

Allende's Chile made the region's strongest documented move toward exit
from the US sphere: copper nationalizations, recognition of Cuba, NAM
rhetoric, and UN-corroborated diplomatic drift (CHL–USA distance 1.89
[1969] → 2.76 [1970], holding ~2.5–2.8; CHL–USSR 2.51 [1969] → 2.08
[1971]). But **zero indicator families were terminated**:
- F1: no exit from the Rio Treaty (ATOP 3075) or OAS — membership
  continuous; no Soviet security guarantee established.
- F2: the trade collapse (share vs USA 0.27 → 0.14, 1970–72) was
  **patron-enforced** — the US credit blockade ("make the economy scream"),
  GSDB-R5 case 147 (destabilization, from 1970) — not a Chilean exit from
  trade ties.
- F3: no bloc exit; no CMEA, no Soviet-bloc institutional tie.
- F4 exists only as the patron's coercion record (147; and 179 from 1974,
  directed at Pinochet).
R2 fails (needs ≥2 of {security guarantee, subsidized trade/aid, bloc
membership} terminated). What remains is a *drift toward exit that was
never consummated* — exactly the E017 class. Coded severance_threat,
**excluded from the Severance regressor** (robustness may include it).

**R4:** n/a (unconsummated). The initiative was the client's (chosen
drift); the patron's response was coercion and, finally, support for the
11 Sep 1973 coup — hierarchy enforcement, recorded as context for the
narrative layer, not as part of the episode. **R6 = 0.**

## Validation

UN ideal points show the drift AND its reversal: post-coup, CHL–USA falls
to 1.08 (1974) and CHL–USSR jumps to 3.35 (1974) — the sphere reasserts
itself. The validation thus supports the *unconsummated* coding: the
distance moved, the memberships never did.
