# Case note — Spain–US 1953 (entry from isolation)

**Episode ID:** E002 | **Status:** calibration episode, in panel (ESP) |
**Coder:** assistant + user, review pending | **Confidence:** high

## The four judgments (CP §6.1)

**1. Membership (R1).** Pre-1953 Spain was in **no sphere**: excluded from the
Marshall Plan and NATO, under UN diplomatic exclusion (1946 resolution,
ambassadors withdrawn). Diplomatic normalization with the US began 1950–51
(Korean War context). No predominant foreign influence before 1953 — this is an
*entry from isolation*, not a sphere switch.

**2. Change (R3 — entry).** Establishment of ≥2 of {security guarantee,
subsidized trade/aid, bloc membership} within 3 years:
- F1 security: **26 September 1953** — Pact of Madrid (three executive
  agreements): US air/naval bases at Rota, Torrejón, Zaragoza, Morón.
  Documentary note: the 1953 agreements were executive agreements, so ATOP does
  not list them; ATOP's first formal US–Spain defense treaty is **3480 (1963
  renewal, defense=1, base=3, milaid=4)**, which formalizes what began in 1953.
  CP §6 F1 explicitly includes "troop presence and bases," so F1 dates to 1953.
- F2 economic: same agreements carry US economic and military aid (initial
  package ≈ $226M; roughly ~1% of Spanish GDP per year in the first years;
  cumulative 1953–61 ≈ $1.5bn across grants and loans — qualitative estimate,
  DOTS/DAC refinement pending).
- F3 bloc (corroborating): **UN admission December 1955** (within the 3-year
  window of 1953); IMF/World Bank 1958, OECD 1959.
Two families established simultaneously in 1953 → **point entry, 1953**
(no interval needed; the core families were established by a single act).

**3. Voluntariness (R4).** **Chosen.** Franco sought legitimacy and resources;
the US sought bases (Cold War). Classic bandwagoning for regime security
(Walt 1987). Not imposed.

**4. Sign (R5).** **Richer sphere, mechanically verified:** US GDP pc 1953 =
16,917 (Maddison 2011$) vs. Europe-region median = 6,594. Entry → richer sphere
→ H2 predicts acceleration following entry. **R6 systemic-shift flag: 0.**

## Validation

- **UN ideal points:** Spain enters the UN in December 1955 already voting close
  to the US (1956: 0.85 vs 1.06; gaps of 0.0–0.9 through the early 1960s) —
  convergence confirmed. (No pre-1956 baseline exists: Spain was not a UN member.)
- **Outcome check (post-hoc, non-contamination rule respected):** growth regime
  shifts from 2.65% (1953–60) to 6.52% (1961–68), a +3.9pp break that *follows*
  the entry (timing rule satisfied; mediated by the 1959 Stabilization Plan).
  **However, no acceleration episode is coded** under the registered thresholds:
  the 1961–68 window crosses the Maddison→WDI source seam (levels jump 5,079 →
  6,285 in 1960 by units change), so the A4 within-source level check correctly
  excludes it; the first fully single-source window (1968–75) fails A3 because
  the prior regime already contains the boom (5.76%).

## Conventions fixed by this episode

1. **Executive agreements count for F1** when they establish bases/troops, even
   if absent from ATOP; the formal treaty codification (1963) is noted as such.
2. **Entry can be a point event** when ≥2 families are established by one act;
   intervals are for gradual processes only.
3. **Spain is the entry-side calibration case** (mirror of Vietnam on the
   severance side): realignment confirmed, outcome positive in sign and timing
   but below the episode threshold. It enters the descriptive layer as an entry
