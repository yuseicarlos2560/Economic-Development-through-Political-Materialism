# Case note — Cuba 1959–1962: paired severance (US) and entry (USSR)

**Episode IDs:** E003 (severance from US sphere), E004 (entry into Soviet sphere)
| **Status:** calibration episodes, in panel (CUB) | **Coder:** assistant + user,
review pending | **Confidence:** high

These must be coded together: the same window (1959–1962) contains a severance
from the old patron and an entry into a new one, and the net outcome cannot be
understood unless both are coded.

## E003 — Severance from the US sphere

**1. Membership (R1), pre-1959.** Cuba inside the US sphere: hegemonic since
1902; F1 Guantánamo base + Rio Treaty (1947, ATOP); F3 OAS founding member
(1948); F2 trade concentrated on the US (~2/3 of Cuban trade; historical
estimates — DOTS sparse pre-1960).

**2. Severance (R2).** Terminations within a 3-year window:
- F2: US trade embargo — sugar quota cancelled July 1960, exports embargoed
  Oct 1960, full embargo **Feb 1962** (GSDB-R5 cases 59, 74, 65: trade 1960,
  financial 1961, full 1962; plus the 1958 arms embargo, case 51).
- F1/F3: diplomatic rupture **Jan 1961**; Cuba expelled from the OAS
  **Jan–Feb 1962** (OAS sanctions follow 1964, case 98).
Interval-coded **[1960, 1962]** (embargo 1960 → OAS expulsion 1962).

**3. Voluntariness (R4).** **Imposed** — the severance was enforced by the
patron through sanctions (R4's second clause). Cuba did not initiate the
economic break; the US escalated it stepwise.

**4. R5 n/a. R6: 0.**

## E004 — Entry into the Soviet sphere

**1. Membership criterion applied to entry (R3).**
- F2: **February 1960** Soviet–Cuban trade pact (sugar for oil, $100M credit);
  by 1961–62 ~80% of Cuban trade with the socialist bloc (historical estimates).
- F1: Soviet military missions, arms deliveries from 1960–61; **no formal
  defense treaty ever** (ATOP has no Cuba–USSR treaty — the shared record is
  the WWII-era alliance, 1942–45). Cuba was never in the Warsaw Pact. This is
  the protocol's informal-sphere case (Keal 1983; Hast 2014): F1 established
  behaviorally (bases/missions/arms), not treaty-wise.
- F3: not applicable within the window (Cuba joins CMEA only in 1972).
Two families established within 1960–61 → entry coded **1960** (point; the pact
and the missions are documented to the month).

**3. Voluntariness (R4).** **Chosen** (balancing against US coercion; Walt
1987), with the entanglement noted: the choice was made under enforced US
severance. Both readings are documented; the coding is *chosen* because the
initiative for the Soviet pact was Havana's.

**4. Sign (R5).** USSR GDP pc 1960 = 6,288 vs. Latin America median = 3,572
(Maddison) → **richer sphere**, mechanically verified. R6: 0.

## Validation

**UN ideal points** — the cleanest validation so far:
- Cuba votes identically to the US through 1958 (1.14 vs 1.14);
- 1959: −0.13 (break); 1960: −1.57; 1961: −2.38 vs USSR −2.40 — **divergence
  from the US and convergence to the USSR, exactly on the coded dates.**

## Outcome check (post-hoc; non-contamination rule respected)

No threshold disruption 1959–1963 (worst year −3.1% in 1962; max drawdown
~1.6% — far below the 15% rule). The imposed severance from the US did **not**
produce a collapse, because the Soviet entry replaced the capability flows
(subsidized sugar/oil) in the same window. This is theoretically central, not a
coding failure: **paired severance+entry is a different treatment than bare
severance**, and the episode table must keep them separable (hence two rows).
The entry's H2 sign prediction (richer sphere → acceleration) is **not**
confirmed in the 1960s (volatile stagnation; no acceleration episode until
1998–2012, which is unrelated). Recorded as a non-confirming case for the entry
sign — the descriptive layer will count it.
