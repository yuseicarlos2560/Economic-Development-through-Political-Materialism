# Case note — Cuba–USSR 1989–1991 (severance by patron collapse)

**Episode ID:** E005 | **Status:** calibration episode, in panel (CUB); the
closing half of the Cuba cycle (E003/E004) | **Coder:** assistant + user,
review pending | **Confidence:** high

## The four judgments (CP §6.1)

**1. Membership (R1), pre-1989.** Cuba deep inside the Soviet sphere:
- F2: Soviet subsidies ≈ 23% of Cuban GDP in 1985–88 (historical estimate used
  in the protocol); subsidized oil-for-sugar at artificial prices.
- F3: CMEA member since 1972.
- F1: Soviet military presence (Lourdes signals facility, training brigade,
  missions) — still no treaty; informal sphere, behaviorally documented.

**2. Severance (R2).**
- F2: 1989–91 — subsidy cuts begin under perestroika; **November 1990** the USSR
  announces the shift to world-market prices and hard-currency settlement from
  **January 1991**; oil deliveries collapse 1990–91.
- F3: **CMEA dissolved June 1991.**
- F1: Soviet brigade withdrawal announced **September 1991** (completed 1993).
All three families terminate within 1989–1991 (< 3 years). Interval-coded
**[1989, 1991]** — earliest defensible: subsidy reductions begin 1989; latest:
CMEA dissolution + world-price shift + troop withdrawal 1991. The patron's
final dissolution (Dec 1991) is the endpoint context, not the severance date.

**3. Voluntariness (R4).** **Imposed** — R4's first clause verbatim: the patron
withdrew and then ceased to exist. GSDB confirms the USSR imposed no sanctions
(the patron collapsed rather than coerced); the *US* tightened its embargo in
the same window (Torricelli Act 1992, GSDB case 443) — context that deepened the
shock, noted but not part of the severance coding.

**4. R5 n/a. R6 systemic-shift flag: 1** — the episode coincides with the
1989–91 change in the global distribution of capabilities (end of bipolarity;
Waltz 1979; Gilpin 1981). This is exactly the case R6 exists for: the flag keeps
"realignment" and "systemic shift" separable in the robustness checks
(exclusion of 1989–91, draft2.tex §5.2).

## Validation

- **UN ideal points:** Cuba–USSR gap ~0.2 in 1986 → ~2.7 by 1991. The
  divergence is driven by the *patron* moving toward the US (Russia −0.09 in
  1990 → +0.73 in 1991 → +1.29 in 1992; Cuba stays ~−2). This is the signature
  of patron withdrawal — it validates the severance and independently supports
  the *imposed* coding (the country did not move; its sphere moved away).

## Outcome check (post-hoc; timing rule examined carefully)

Screen-coded disruption D019: peak 1985, trough 1993, depth −36.8%. Year detail:
flat fluctuation 1985–89 (rectification period; worst year −3.3%), then the
collapse **1990 −3.8%, 1991 −11.4%, 1992 −12.1%, 1993 −15.3%**. The episode's
peak year (1985) predates the severance, but the *contraction phase*
(1990–93, −33% in four years) follows and overlaps the severance window
[1989, 1991]. Timing rule: **satisfied for the collapse proper**. Honest
caveat recorded: growth was already stagnant 1985–89, so the peak-to-trough
depth slightly overstates the post-severance contraction; the two phases must
be distinguished in the case analysis.

**H2 negative sign: strongly confirmed** — this is the theory's showcase case
(imposed severance, intact power structure, threshold collapse following).

## Conventions fixed

   the timing rule is evaluated against the contraction phase, not the peak year.
2. **Patron-driven divergence counts** as UN-voting validation for severance,
