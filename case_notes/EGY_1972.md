# Case note — Egypt 1972–1979: chosen severance (USSR) then entry (US)

**Episode IDs:** E006 (severance from USSR), E007 (entry into US sphere) |
**Status:** calibration episodes (Egypt is not in the 73-country panel) |
**Coder:** assistant + user, review pending | **Confidence:** high

The mirror image of Cuba 1960: there the patron imposed the severance; here the
*client* initiates it. The contrast is what makes Egypt calibration-valuable.

## E006 — Severance from the Soviet sphere

**1. Membership (R1), pre-1972.** Egypt in the Soviet sphere since 1955 (arms
deals) and deepened after 1967: F1 — ~20,000 Soviet advisors, air-defense
operation, Treaty of Friendship and Cooperation May 1971 (ATOP 3670: nonagg +
consul + milaid); F2 — credits, arms on concessional terms; UN voting aligned
(1969: Egypt −1.67 vs USSR −1.64).

**2. Severance (R2).**
- F1: **July 1972** — Sadat expels the Soviet military mission (~20,000
  advisors); arms flows halt. **March 1976** — Egypt unilaterally abrogates the
  1971 treaty (ATOP 3670: exit 1976, terminated by renunciation).
- F2: Soviet credits and concessional supplies end; debt-service demands follow.
- F4 record: **GSDB-R5 case 198 — Soviet sanctions on Egypt 1976–84 (arms +
  financial)** — retaliation by the ex-patron; coded as an F4 record, it does
  not change the voluntariness coding (the initiative was Egypt's).
Interval **[1972, 1976]**: earliest = mission expulsion; latest = treaty
abrogation. Two families (F1, F2) terminate within the window.

**3. Voluntariness (R4).** **Chosen** — the client initiated the break (Walt
1987: rebalancing toward a better-resourced patron). Counterpart to Cuba's
*imposed* 1960 severance.

**4. R5 n/a. R6: 0.**

## E007 — Entry into the US sphere

**1. Entry (R3).**
- F2: US–Egypt relations restored **February 1974**; US aid resumes 1974–75
  (~$250M in 1975, PL-480 food aid; rising toward ~$1bn/yr by decade's end).
- F1 (informal, no treaty — ATOP has no US–Egypt defense treaty): security
  partnership through the 1974–75 disengagement agreements; first arms sales
  mid-1970s; consolidated at **Camp David 1978** and the 1979 assistance
  package (Bright Star exercises from 1980).
Interval-coded **[1974, 1979]**: earliest = relations + aid 1974; latest
defensible = Camp David consolidation 1978/79.

**3. Voluntariness (R4).** **Chosen** (both sides).

**4. Sign (R5).** US GDP pc 1974 = 26,286 vs Egypt 2,099 (Maddison) → richer
sphere. (Egypt is outside the sample regions, so the regional-median comparison
is illustrative; the gap is too large to be sensitive to the reference set.)
R6: 0.

## Validation

**UN ideal points** trace the transition year by year: Egypt −0.63 (1972) →
−0.13 (1974) → +0.04 (1979) → 0.00 (1982), while the USSR stays ~−2.2 and the
US ~+2.3. Egypt leaves the Soviet position starting 1972–74 and approaches —
without fully reaching — the US position by 1979–80. Both the divergence
(severance) and the convergence (entry) are visible, on the coded dates.

## Outcome check (post-hoc)

**No disruption follows the severance** — growth is flat 1972–74 (war economy),
then accelerates 1975–80 (7.9%, 13.0%, 10.0%, 4.3%, 4.7%, 7.2%; Maddison). The
pattern matches the framework's paired logic (as in Cuba 1960): the entry into
the richer sphere followed the chosen severance within ~2 years and replaced
the capability flows — severance alone disrupts, severance-then-entry does not.
Egypt is outside the panel (no screen episode); as calibration it supports the
mean-growth sign of H2 and the asymmetry between bare and paired severance.

## Conventions fixed

1. **Client-initiated breaks code as *chosen***; ex-patron retaliation
   (GSDB case 198) is an F4 record, not a voluntariness override.
2. **Entry intervals** span from first establishment (aid/relations) to
   consolidation (Camp David); the endpoint robustness rule applies.
